The generalized unscented transform

The generalized unscented transform of Ebeigbe et al. places 2d + 1 sigma points, as many as the unscented transform does, but matches more of the input: its mean and covariance, and the third and fourth central moments of each of its components. It suits an input that is not normal, skewed or heavy-tailed, whose moments are known. For a normal input it matches the fourth moment too, which the unscented transform with its default parameters does not.

using MessagePassingRulesApproximations
import MessagePassingRulesApproximations: GenUnscented, GenConstrainedUnscented, sigma_points_weights

GenUnscented and GenConstrainedUnscented are public, not exported: RxGPs exports names of its own for its copy of the transform, which would otherwise clash.

The sigma points

For a scalar input with mean $m$, variance $σ^2$ and central moments $S = \mathrm{E}[(x - m)^3]$ and $K = \mathrm{E}[(x - m)^4]$, the points are $m$, $m - uσ$ and $m + vσ$, with

\[u = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(-s + \sqrt{4k - 3s^2}\right), \quad v = u + s, \qquad w^+ = \frac{1}{v (u + v)}, \quad w^- = \frac{v}{u} w^+, \quad w_0 = 1 - w^- - w^+,\]

where $s = S/σ^3$ and $k = K/σ^4$ are the standardised skewness and kurtosis. A vector input takes them per component, along the columns of the Cholesky factor of its covariance. A gamma's points reproduce its four moments:

α, θ = 2.0, 1.5
m, V, S, K = α * θ, α * θ^2, 2α * θ^3, 3α * (α + 2) * θ^4   # a gamma's central moments
points, weights = sigma_points_weights(GenUnscented(), m, V, S, K)
[sum(w * (x - m)^k for (w, x) in zip(weights, points)) for k in 2:4], (V, S, K)
([4.5, 13.500000000000007, 121.5], (4.5, 13.5, 121.5))

Without S and K the input is normal, with no skewness and $K = 3σ^4$. A quartic shows the gain over the unscented transform, which is exact to degree three for a normal:

f(x) = x^4
m, v = 0.5, 0.3
(exact = m^4 + 6m^2 * v + 3v^2, generalized = expectation(GenUnscented(), f, m, v), unscented = expectation(Unscented(), f, m, v))
(exact = 0.7825, generalized = 0.7824999999999999, unscented = 0.5125000899861334)

Bounds

GenConstrainedUnscented(lower, upper) keeps the points within bounds, for an input that cannot leave them, a positive one for instance. A point that would fall outside moves towards the mean; the mean and covariance are still matched:

points, weights = sigma_points_weights(GenConstrainedUnscented(0.0, Inf), 1.0, 4.0)
(points = points, mean = sum(weights .* points), variance = sum(weights .* (points .- 1) .^ 2))
(points = (1.0, 0.5, 1.5), mean = 1.0, variance = 4.0)

API

MessagePassingRulesApproximations.GenUnscented — Type
GenUnscented()

The generalized unscented transform (Ebeigbe et al., 2021): 2d + 1 sigma points whose weights match the input's mean and covariance and the third and fourth central moments of each of its components, so it is exact for polynomials of degree four in each component. GenUT and GenUnscentedTransform are aliases.

Its measure is given by params = (m, V, S, K): the mean, the covariance, and the vectors of the components' third and fourth central moments, S = E[(x - m)³] and K = E[(x - m)⁴], numbers for a scalar input. Several independent inputs take tuples, (means, covs, skews, kurts). Without S and K, params = (m, V) or (means, covs), the input is normal: S = 0 and K = 3σ⁴. A K below the smallest one the skewness allows is raised to it.

It is used through approximate, forward_statistics, expectation and approximate_meancov, with their in-place forms; sigma_points_weights returns its points. GenConstrainedUnscented keeps the points within bounds.

Ebeigbe, Berry, Norton and Simon (2021), A generalized unscented transformation for probability distributions, arXiv:2104.01958; Physical Review E 111 (2025).

Examples

julia> import MessagePassingRulesApproximations: GenUnscented

julia> m, V = approximate(GenUnscented(), x -> x^2, 1.0, 2.0);   # x ~ N(1, 2): E[x²] = 3

julia> m ≈ 3.0 && V ≈ 4 * 1.0^2 * 2.0 + 2 * 2.0^2   # Var[x²] = 4m²v + 2v²
true

MessagePassingRulesApproximations.GenConstrainedUnscented — Type
GenConstrainedUnscented(lower, upper; shrink = 0.5)

The generalized unscented transform, GenUnscented, with its sigma points kept within the bounds lower and upper: numbers for a scalar input, vectors for a vector one, and tuples of those, one entry per input, for several; -Inf and Inf leave a component unbounded. A point that would fall outside moves towards the mean, to shrink times its distance from the mean to the bound, and the point opposite moves so that the skewness stays matched when it can. The mean and covariance are matched whatever the bounds; the mean must lie within them.

GenCUT is an alias. Its parameters are those of GenUnscented.

Examples

julia> import MessagePassingRulesApproximations: GenConstrainedUnscented, sigma_points_weights

julia> points, weights = sigma_points_weights(GenConstrainedUnscented(0.0, Inf), 1.0, 4.0);

julia> all(>=(0), points) && sum(weights) ≈ 1 && sum(weights .* points) ≈ 1.0
true

MessagePassingRulesApproximations.sigma_points_weights — Method
sigma_points_weights(method::GenUnscented, m, V[, S, K]) -> (points, weights)

The 2d + 1 sigma points of the generalized unscented transform for the mean m, covariance V and, optionally, the components' third and fourth central moments S and K (a normal's without), and their weights, which serve both the mean and the covariance: tuples for a scalar input, vectors otherwise. The first point is the mean; then come the d points below it and the d above. The same for GenConstrainedUnscented.
