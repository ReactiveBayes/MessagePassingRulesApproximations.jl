Gauss–Laguerre quadrature

Gauss–Laguerre quadrature approximates an expectation under a gamma distribution by a weighted sum of the function at fixed points. It suits a positive quantity, a rate or a precision, whose belief is a gamma.

The method

For a gamma with shape $α$ and scale $θ$, the substitution $x = θ t$ turns the expectation into an integral against $t^{α - 1} e^{-t}$, which the generalised Gauss–Laguerre rule with p nodes $t_i$ and weights $w_i$ evaluates:

\[\mathrm{E}[f(x)] = \frac{1}{Γ(α)} \int_0^∞ f(θ t)\, t^{α - 1} e^{-t} \, \mathrm{d}t ≈ \sum_{i=1}^{p} \bar w_i f(θ t_i), \qquad \bar w_i = \frac{w_i}{\sum_j w_j}.\]

The nodes are the roots of the generalised Laguerre polynomial of degree p, so the sum is exact when f is a polynomial of degree up to 2p - 1. The rule depends on the shape, so its nodes are computed on every call: they are the eigenvalues of the Jacobi matrix of the Laguerre polynomials (Golub and Welsch), found in place and polished by Newton's method, so a call allocates nothing, whatever the shape; sixteen nodes cost a few microseconds. An exponential with rate $λ$ is a gamma of shape 1 and scale 1 / λ.

Five points reproduce the first nine raw moments of a gamma, $\mathrm{E}[x^k] = θ^k α (α + 1) ⋯ (α + k - 1)$:

using MessagePassingRulesApproximations

gl = GaussLaguerreQuadrature(5)
α, θ = 2.5, 0.8
[(expectation(gl, x -> x^k, α, θ), θ^k * prod(α + j for j in 0:(k - 1))) for k in 1:4]
4-element Vector{Tuple{Float64, Float64}}:
 (1.9999999999999996, 2.0)
 (5.599999999999998, 5.600000000000001)
 (20.15999999999998, 20.160000000000004)
 (88.70399999999988, 88.70400000000002)

A function that is not smooth at zero, such as $\log x$, converges slowly for a shape below one, where the gamma's density is singular there; it converges fast for larger shapes.

Through a function, and reweighted

The pushforward of a gamma through a function, as a mean and variance, is approximate; the moments of a gamma reweighted by a likelihood are approximate_meancov, in log space with a LogIntegrand:

precision = 2.0, 0.5                      # a gamma belief on a precision
loglikelihood(τ) = 0.5 * log(τ) - τ * 1.7  # N(y | 0, 1 / τ) for an observation y with y² / 2 = 1.7
(
    pushforward = approximate(gl, τ -> 1 / sqrt(τ), precision...),
    posterior = approximate_meancov(glquadrature(20), LogIntegrand(loglikelihood), precision...),
)
(pushforward = (1.2123130408541303, 0.19696375764167773), posterior = (0.6754590623953867, 0.1827165111815368))

The posterior is a gamma with shape $α + 1/2$ and rate $1/θ + 1.7$: mean $2.5 / 3.7 ≈ 0.68$.

API

MessagePassingRulesApproximations.GaussLaguerreQuadrature — Type
GaussLaguerreQuadrature(p::Int)

Gauss–Laguerre quadrature with p points, for expectations under a gamma distribution given by its shape α and scale θ: params = (shape, scale), an exponential with rate λ being (1, 1 / λ). The points are the nodes of the generalised Laguerre rule for the weight t^(α - 1) e^(-t), mapped to x = θ t, so the rule is exact for polynomials of degree up to 2p - 1 under the gamma. It is univariate.

The nodes depend on the shape: they are computed on every call, in place, as the eigenvalues of the Laguerre polynomials' Jacobi matrix polished by Newton's method, so the in-place operations allocate nothing for any shape. In the unlikely case that the eigenvalue iteration does not converge, FastGaussQuadrature computes them, allocating.

It is used through expectation, approximate, forward_statistics and approximate_meancov. glquadrature(p) builds the same.

Examples

julia> gl = GaussLaguerreQuadrature(5);

julia> expectation(gl, x -> x, 2.0, 3.0) ≈ 6.0   # the mean of a gamma, αθ
true

julia> expectation(gl, x -> x^2, 2.0, 3.0) ≈ 2.0 * 3.0 * 3.0^2   # E[x²] = α(α + 1)θ²
true
